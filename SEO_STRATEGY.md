# SEO_STRATEGY.md - Local SEO Execution Plan v1.0 (Claude-assisted)

## Purpose
Build a practical, high-velocity local SEO system that prioritizes **real ranking levers first**, then compounds visibility through consistent execution.

---

## Strategic objective
1. Increase map-pack visibility for high-intent local queries.
2. Increase GBP conversion rate (calls, direction requests, site visits).
3. Build a repeatable weekly operating system that can be executed quickly with AI-assisted research and synthesis.

---

## Core position
Use AI (Claude or equivalent) for data gathering, structure, and draft generation.
Keep human judgment for:
- market nuance,
- prioritization,
- offer positioning,
- and final decision-making.

> AI gathers and organizes. Human decides and executes.

## Priority stack (what to do first)

## Phase 1 - Foundation (highest impact, fastest wins)
1. **GBP Primary Category Audit** _(HIGH confidence)_
   - Validate primary category is the most specific, accurate match for the business's core service.
   - Compare against top map-pack competitors by target keyword. If top 3 all use a more specific primary category, strongly consider switching.
2. **GBP Secondary Category Audit** _(HIGH confidence)_
   - Add all relevant secondary categories that represent real services offered.
   - Remove any secondary categories that don't represent actual services (dilution risk).
   - Check for newly added Google categories quarterly - Google adds new categories regularly.
3. **Services Section Optimization** _(HIGH confidence)_
   - List all real services using both pre-defined and custom service items.
   - Write natural descriptions (2-3 sentences) including service type + service area/location language.
   - Custom services are indexed - don't limit to Google's dropdown suggestions.
4. **GBP Attributes Audit** _(MEDIUM ranking / MEDIUM-HIGH conversion)_
   - Build competitor attribute matrix; identify baseline attributes shared by top competitors.
   - Fill all truthful, applicable attributes — impacts filter eligibility (can determine inclusion in filtered searches like "wheelchair accessible [service] near me") and conversion trust signals.
   - Pay special attention to identity attributes (woman-owned, veteran-led, LGBTQ+ friendly) and service attributes (free estimates, accepts credit cards) — these are growing in search filter usage.
   - Re-audit quarterly as Google regularly expands the attribute taxonomy.

## Phase 2 - Review engine + conversion
5. **Review Velocity Program** _(HIGH confidence)_
   - Benchmark current velocity (30/60/90-day) against top 3 map-pack competitors.
   - Set monthly review acquisition targets to match or exceed competitor velocity.
   - Create a systematic ask process (post-service email/SMS with direct review link).
   - Coach customers to mention what was done and where (not keyword scripts - natural descriptions).
6. **Review Response System** _(LOW for ranking [unproven as ranking signal], HIGH for conversion [proven consumer trust effect])_
   - Build response templates by rating band (5, 4, 3, 1-2 stars).
   - Keep responses human, specific, and timely (respond within 24-48 hours).
   - Mention service and location naturally in responses — primarily for reader context and conversion, not as a ranking tactic (keyword weight in responses is unproven).
   - Respond because ~88% of consumers prefer businesses that respond (BrightLocal 2024), not for ranking movement.
7. **GBP Description Optimization** _(MEDIUM confidence)_
   - Create 2-3 tested variants (ranking-leaning, conversion-leaning, balanced).
   - Rotate and evaluate by impressions/actions. Primary impact is on CTR, not ranking position.
8. **GBP Q&A Seeding** _(MEDIUM confidence)_
   - Seed 5-10 genuine, common customer questions with clear answers.
   - Include natural service/location language. Q&A content is indexed by Google.

## Phase 3 - Ongoing activity engine
9. **GBP Posts Calendar (8 weeks)** _(LOW ranking / MEDIUM conversion)_
   - 2-3 posts/week: service highlights, proof, neighborhood relevance, seasonal offers.
   - Prioritize **Offer** and **Event** post types over generic Updates — Offers get CTA buttons and dedicated display; Events get date-based visibility. Updates are lowest-priority.
   - Posts expire after ~7 days — consistency matters for the freshness signal but don't over-invest expecting ranking gains. Controlled experiments (Sterling Sky 2023-2024) show no significant ranking movement from posts alone.
   - Primary value: conversion (driving calls/clicks from active searchers) and activity trust signal.
10. **GBP Photo Program** _(LOW-MEDIUM ranking / MEDIUM-HIGH conversion)_
    - Weekly uploads of real operational images (before/after, team, on-site work, interior/exterior).
    - Prefer geo-tagged, original photos with intact EXIF metadata — location/timestamp data provides additional location confirmation to Google.
    - Google Vision AI parses image content and can identify business type, services, and premises quality — use authentic operational photos, not stock images.
    - Photo quality and relevance > raw volume. Don't chase the "100+ photos" correlation without context — that metric is confounded by business maturity/quality.
    - User-generated photos from customers carry implicit trust; encourage but don't fabricate.
11. **Core Citation / NAP Audit** _(table-stakes, not a growth lever)_
    - Verify NAP consistency across Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, and top industry directories.
    - Fix inconsistencies. Do not invest in mass directory submission - diminishing returns.

## Evidence confidence (updated 2026-03-16)

### Quick-reference hierarchy (ranking impact)
> **Categories** (primary > secondary) → **Review signals** (velocity/recency > content relevance > diversity > owner response) → **Services section** → **Attributes** (filter eligibility + moderate ranking) → **Description / Q&A** → **Posts / Photos / Citations** (low ranking, useful for conversion)

### High confidence levers (PROVEN — directly observable or confirmed by controlled tests / consistent survey consensus)
- **Primary category selection** - Single highest-impact GBP setting. The primary category alone determines initial keyword eligibility for map pack. Confirmed by Whitespark annual ranking factor surveys (2023, 2024) where GBP signals (led by category) account for ~32% of map pack ranking weight.
- **Secondary category coverage** - Adding relevant secondaries expands keyword surface. Over-adding irrelevant ones dilutes relevance. Check quarterly for newly added Google categories.
- **Service section completeness** - Services are now indexed by Google for keyword matching. Custom services (not just pre-defined dropdown items) are crawled. Descriptions should include natural service + location language. Weight of this signal has increased in recent survey data.
- **Proximity to searcher** - Remains the #1 single factor overall. No amount of optimization fully overcomes geographic distance for local queries. Strategy must acknowledge service-area realities.

### High confidence levers (LIKELY PROVEN — strong practitioner consensus + observable correlations, but limited controlled experiments)
- **Review velocity** - Rate of new reviews over 30/60/90-day windows is consistently ranked as a top review sub-signal in Whitespark ranking factor surveys (2023, 2024). Practitioner consensus (Darren Shaw / Whitespark, Joy Hawkins / Sterling Sky) holds that steady acquisition outperforms burst patterns and that velocity matters more than raw total once a baseline threshold is met. **What is proven:** Google tracks review recency and freshness — this is directly observable in how recent reviews are surfaced prominently and in the "review justification" snippets. Google's spam detection flags unnatural velocity spikes, confirming they model velocity patterns. **What is likely but not experimentally isolated:** the exact ranking weight of velocity vs. total count vs. average rating; whether Google applies a specific mathematical decay function (half-life, linear) to older reviews; the precise velocity needed to compete in any given market. **Practical guidance unchanged:** benchmark 30/60/90-day velocity against top 3 map-pack competitors and set monthly targets to match or exceed. Steady, authentic acquisition beats sporadic bursts.
- **Review recency** - Google increasingly surfaces recent reviews and factors freshness into ranking. A listing with steady monthly reviews outperforms one with the same total count but stale activity. This is observable in how Google highlights "New" reviews and surfaces recent ones in knowledge panels. **Unproven:** the specific decay rate or lookback window Google uses for recency weighting.
- **Review content relevance** — **What is proven (directly observable):** Google extracts keywords from review text and displays them as "review justifications" — blue highlighted snippets shown below listings in map pack results. Google also surfaces "known for" topic tags derived from review content (e.g., "known for fast service", "great for families"). These features are visible in live SERPs. **What is likely:** reviews mentioning specific services contribute to keyword relevance for those service queries — widely observed by practitioners (listings with reviews mentioning "roof repair" ranking for "roof repair near me") but hard to isolate experimentally because businesses good at a service naturally receive reviews mentioning it. Location mentions (city, neighborhood) in reviews likely contribute to geographic relevance. **What is unproven:** the exact ranking weight of review-text keywords vs. other signals (categories, services); whether Google differentially weights reviews by reviewer history/authority; whether sentiment analysis of text (beyond star rating) feeds into ranking. **Guidance unchanged:** coach customers to describe what was done and where in their own words. Do not solicit keyword-stuffed or templated reviews — Google's review spam systems flag unnatural patterns, and there's no evidence coached content outperforms natural descriptions once you control for underlying service quality.

### Medium confidence levers
- **Attribute optimization** - Filling all valid, truthful attributes provides relevance signals and filter eligibility. Direct ranking weight is moderate (Whitespark 2023/2024 surveys place attributes as a secondary GBP signal below categories/reviews), but the practical impact comes in two forms: (1) **Filter eligibility** — attributes like "wheelchair accessible", "woman-owned", "free Wi-Fi", "LGBTQ+ friendly" determine whether a listing appears in filtered searches, which is effectively a ranking benefit for those query variants (Sterling Sky has confirmed attribute-driven filtered inclusion in controlled tests); (2) **Conversion trust signals** — attributes displayed on the listing act as decision-making shortcuts that reduce friction for searchers. Google's attribute taxonomy continues to expand (health/safety, identity, service-specific), so quarterly attribute audits remain important. **Ranking confidence: MEDIUM. Conversion confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.**
- **GBP description optimization** - Description is indexed but carries less direct ranking weight than categories/services. Primary value is conversion (CTR from listing). A/B testing variants is useful for conversion, less so for ranking movement.
- **Review diversity / sentiment profile** - **What is likely:** a natural distribution (not all 5-star) appears more trustworthy to both users and Google's review spam detection. Listings with suspiciously uniform ratings may face additional scrutiny or automated review removal. Google's increased fake review enforcement (2024-2025) specifically targets patterns of uniform 5-star reviews. **What is unproven:** whether Google algorithmically rewards sentiment diversity as a positive ranking signal vs. simply penalizing suspicious uniformity.
- **Behavioral / engagement signals** - Click-through rate, direction requests, calls-from-listing, and dwell time on GBP are increasingly correlated with rankings. Hard to manipulate directly; best addressed by improving listing quality and conversion elements.
- **GBP Q&A section** - Overlooked by most businesses. Q&A content is indexed and can provide keyword relevance. Seed your own Q&A with genuine, useful questions and answers.

### Lower direct-ranking confidence (still useful for conversion/trust)
- **Review response text** — **What is proven:** Owner responses strongly influence consumer behavior. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey (2024) found ~88% of consumers are "more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews" and ~57% said they would be "unlikely to use a business that doesn't respond to reviews." Responses are publicly visible and serve as customer-service trust signals. **What is likely:** responding demonstrates active listing management (an engagement/activity signal), and Google can parse response text — but this is a minor signal at best. **What is unproven (and commonly overstated):** (1) owner responses as a direct ranking signal — Whitespark ranking factor surveys place "owner response to reviews" in the lower tier of review sub-signals, and no controlled experiments have demonstrated that responding to reviews causes ranking improvement; (2) keywords in owner responses carrying ranking weight — this is one of the most commonly repeated but least substantiated local SEO claims; Joy Hawkins / Sterling Sky have noted Google can index response text but have not shown ranking movement from it; (3) response speed (e.g., within 24 hours vs. 1 week) as a ranking signal — this is a conversion/trust best practice but not a confirmed ranking lever. **Confidence: LOW for ranking, HIGH for conversion/trust.** Respond because it wins customers, not because it moves rankings.
- **GBP posts** - Multiple controlled experiments (Sterling Sky / Joy Hawkins, 2023-2024) have failed to demonstrate meaningful direct ranking movement from post activity. The Whitespark ranking factor surveys consistently rank posts near the bottom of the factor hierarchy. Posts expire from prominent display after ~7 days. Offer and Event post types receive slightly more prominent placement than generic Update posts (dedicated sections in the knowledge panel vs. lower-visibility Updates tab). Posts are best understood as: (a) a minor freshness signal confirming the business is active, (b) a conversion tool — Offer posts include CTA buttons and can drive direct action, (c) a trust signal for searchers evaluating whether a business is currently operating. Do not invest disproportionate time here expecting ranking gains. **Ranking confidence: LOW. Conversion confidence: MEDIUM.**
- **Photo activity** - Correlation between photo volume and engagement metrics is well-documented (Google's own data: businesses with photos receive ~42% more direction requests and ~35% more website click-throughs; BrightLocal: businesses with 100+ photos show dramatically higher engagement) — but these correlations are strongly confounded by the fact that more established, higher-quality businesses naturally have more photos. No controlled experiments have isolated photo uploads as a direct ranking lever. That said, two emerging technical signals are worth noting: (1) **Geo-tagged EXIF data** — original photos with location/timestamp metadata provide additional location confirmation to Google's systems; (2) **Google Vision AI content parsing** — Google processes image content and can identify business type, services offered, and premises quality, which may feed into relevance scoring over time. Practically: prioritize authentic, operational photos (before/after, team on-site, interior/exterior) over stock images. Photo quality and relevance matter more than raw volume. **Ranking confidence: LOW-MEDIUM (emerging Vision AI signals may increase this). Conversion confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (visual trust is a strong decision-making factor).**
- **Citation volume / directory listings** - NAP consistency is table-stakes (must be correct) but citation building as a ranking tactic has continued to decline in relative importance per Whitespark surveys. Focus on core directories (Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, industry-specific) rather than volume.

### Emerging considerations (monitor, don't over-invest yet)
- **AI Overviews / SGE** - Google's AI-generated summaries are changing how local results appear in SERPs. Local packs still appear but sometimes below AI overviews. Monitor click-through impact.
- **GBP product listings** - Gaining visibility for applicable business types. If the business sells products, populate this section.
- **"Known for" / topic extraction from reviews** - Google is surfacing review-derived topic tags ("known for fast service", "great for families"). Natural review quality drives these, not direct optimization.
- **Google review spam enforcement** - Google has increased automated fake review detection and removal. Relying on authentic review acquisition is now both ethically and tactically correct.

## Hard constraints & risk controls (policy-grounded)

### Absolute prohibitions (violation = suspension risk)
1. **No fake or incentivized reviews.** Google explicitly states: "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited." Violation triggers listing suspension and review removal. _(Source: GBP Help — Tips to get more reviews, answer/3474122, accessed 2026-03-16)_
2. **No review gating.** Do not selectively solicit reviews only from satisfied customers while discouraging or intercepting negative feedback. Google's prohibited content policies and FTC guidelines both flag this practice. _(Source: Google Maps User Generated Content Policy, answer/7400114; FTC endorsement guidelines)_
3. **No business name keyword stuffing.** GBP guidelines require: "Your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers." Adding service keywords, location names, or marketing slogans to the business name field violates policy and risks suspension. _(Source: GBP Help — Guidelines for representing your business on Google, answer/3038177, accessed 2026-03-16)_
4. **No duplicate listings.** "There should only be one profile per business, as this can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search." _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3038177)_
5. **No links in the business description.** "No links of any type are allowed" in the description field. _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3038177)_
6. **No promotional pricing in description.** The description must not "focus on special promotions, prices, and offer sales." _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3038177)_

### Risk controls (tactic-level guardrails)
7. **Review solicitation must be neutral and unscripted.** Ask all customers equally; do not provide keyword scripts or coached language. Google's spam detection flags unnatural review patterns and templated content. Coach customers to describe what was done and where in their own words only.
8. **Owner review responses must not contain promotions.** Google's review reply guidelines: "avoid using your response to offer deals or promotions." Responses are for customer relationship, not marketing. _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3474122)_
9. **Never share reviewer private information in responses.** "Never share a reviewer's private information or engage in personal attacks." _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3474122)_
10. **Category selection: fewest accurate categories.** "Choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business." Adding irrelevant categories for keyword coverage dilutes relevance and risks policy action. _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3038177)_
11. **All attributes must be truthful.** Only fill attributes that accurately describe the business. Claiming false identity/accessibility/service attributes for filter eligibility is deceptive content.
12. **Description must be factual and relevant.** No "low-quality, irrelevant, or distracting content" including "misspellings, gimmicky character use, gibberish." _(Source: GBP Help, answer/3038177)_
13. **Photos must be authentic.** Do not use stock images, fabricated user-generated photos, or manipulated EXIF metadata. Google Vision AI parses photo content for authenticity.

### Strategy-level constraints (unchanged)
14. Claims and timelines remain realistic (no guaranteed "$100k/month" narrative in strategy docs).
15. Changes must be measurable against baseline metrics.
16. Focus on compounding execution, not one-off hacks.

## Operating cadence
- **Weekly:**
  - 1 competitor scan refresh
  - 1 services/attributes/category drift check
  - review velocity check + response SLA
  - post + photo execution
- **Biweekly:**
  - description/post theme optimization
- **Monthly:**
  - performance review + priority rebalance
- **Quarterly:**
  - category taxonomy check (Google adds new categories regularly)
  - attribute taxonomy re-audit (Google expands attributes regularly)
  - evidence confidence reassessment (re-run research cycle if warranted)

1. Map-pack visibility by target keyword cluster
2. Calls / direction requests / site clicks from GBP (conversion actions)
3. Review velocity (30/60/90-day) vs top 3 map-pack competitors
4. % reviews responded within 48-hour SLA
5. Service-area query coverage (impressions by location terms)
6. GBP completeness score (primary/secondary categories, services, attributes, Q&A, description — all filled and accurate)

- Week 1: primary + secondary category audit, services section build-out, attributes audit
- Week 2: review velocity benchmarking, review acquisition process setup, response templates

- Week 4: posts + photo system launch, first KPI baseline measurement, priority adjustment
